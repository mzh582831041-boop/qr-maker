No. Once you download your QR code as a PNG image, it is permanent. The QR code itself does not expire — it only depends on whether the URL or information you encoded remains accessible.

Can I scan QR codes without an app?

Yes. Modern smartphones (iPhone and Android) have built-in QR code scanners in the camera app. Simply open your camera and point it at the QR code to scan.

Technical Details

QR codes generated by this tool use the widely-adopted QR Code specification (ISO/IEC 18004). The generated PNG images are standard format compatible with all QR code scanners. Error correction level H (highest) is used by default, allowing up to 30% of the code to be damaged while remaining scannable.

All processing happens in your browser using the qrcodejs library. No data is uploaded to any server. Your privacy is always protected.

Pro Tips for Better QR Codes

  • Test before printing: Always scan your QR code with multiple devices before mass printing. Different scanners may interpret codes differently.
  • Maintain contrast: Ensure sufficient contrast between the QR code color and background. Dark codes on light backgrounds scan best.
  • Leave margin space: Keep a clear margin (called quiet zone) around your QR code equal to at least 4 modules (the smallest square unit) for reliable scanning.
  • Use short URLs: If encoding a URL, use a URL shortener first. Shorter content creates simpler QR codes that scan more reliably.
  • Avoid low-contrast colors: Light colors like yellow, pink, or light blue on white backgrounds will not scan. Stick to dark foreground colors.

Common QR Code Mistakes to Avoid

QR code is too small

A QR code should be at least 2x2 cm (0.8x0.8 inches) for print. For digital use, ensure it is large enough to scan comfortably on mobile screens.

QR code on curved surfaces

Printing QR codes on curved surfaces like coffee mugs or bottles can distort the pattern and prevent scanning. Consider using a flat label or testing before production.

No fallback for dynamic content

If you expect the destination URL to change, consider using a dynamic QR code service that allows you to update the link without reprinting. Our static QR codes are permanent and cannot be changed.

What Is a QR Code?

A QR (Quick Response) code is a type of matrix barcode that stores information in both horizontal and vertical patterns. Unlike traditional barcodes that hold up to 20 characters, QR codes can store up to 4,296 alphanumeric characters, making them ideal for URLs, contact information, and text messages.

QR codes were invented in 1994 by Denso Wave, a Japanese automotive parts manufacturer. Today they are used globally for marketing, payments, authentication, and information sharing. Modern smartphones can scan QR codes directly through their camera apps without additional software.

How QR Codes Work

When you scan a QR code, your device reads the arrangement of black and white squares. The three large squares in the corners help the scanner identify the code's orientation. The remaining pattern encodes the actual data using error correction technology that allows the code to remain scannable even if partially damaged (up to 30% for the highest correction level).

Our QR code generator uses error correction level H (the highest), ensuring your codes remain scannable even with minor damage or distortion.
